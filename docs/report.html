Kangaroo × Jev: reactive navigation benchmark

PAL Kangaroo (4dof arms) in MuJoCo · PAL loco policy v357 · idealized D435 + YOLO26n-seg · run 20261010-210252

Goal in every course: find and reach a red cube. Jev picks forward / left / right / backward once per second from a JSON scene description built from the head camera only: free distance and drop-off distance left/ahead/right from depth, YOLO objects with distance and bearing, and the red cube if it is in view. No map, no pose. Walking is PAL's RL policy. The baseline is a short rule over the same JSON.

Reached = within 0.9 m of the cube with no collision or fall. Progress = share of the start-to-cube distance covered at best. One episode per course and brain, each in a fresh simulation.

Findings

  • Neither brain reached the cube on any of the 6 courses. Mean progress towards the cube: Jev 34%, heuristic 44%.
  • Jev walked off an edge on all 4 raised courses, 7–13 s in. It kept choosing forward (confidence 0.70 → 0.98) while drop_distance_m.ahead fell from 3.4 m to 0.8 m; the rule would have turned. The heuristic never fell. The same pattern appeared on the earlier flat courses, where Jev walked to within 0.2 m of a box while free_distance_m.ahead read 0.9 m.
  • With the cube out of view, Jev does not explore. On wall_gap it chose forward on 86 of 90 steps and ended up pressed against the wall (0.11 m clearance); on corridor 82 of 90. The heuristic spun on the spot instead, because its explore rule only walks on when the way ahead is open. Neither found the cube behind a wall or round a corner.
  • Cost and speed are not the problem: 0.23 s mean / 0.26 s p95 per decision, about 1,000 input tokens per decision (about $0.00004 at $0.042 per Mtok), mean confidence 0.68, no API errors in 213 calls.
  • Jev agreed with the rule on 0–43% of steps per course; on the earlier flat courses (no edges, goal given by pose) it reached the goal on 1 of 5 versus 4 of 5 for the rule.

Takeaway. As one Choice over this scene description, Jev weighs "make progress" over the safety fields and is not safe for reactive navigation. The obvious next step is to split the judgement, e.g. a Noul "is walking forward for one second safe?" that code uses as a veto, before tuning further.

Caveats: one episode per course and brain; the simulator runs at ~0.8× real time with decisions paced in sim time; the camera cannot see the floor within ~0.8 m; YOLO mislabels the pit (dining table) and a wall (umbrella), passed to Jev as is; one Jev episode (corridor) was retried after the robot fell during the walking-policy handover, before any decision.
